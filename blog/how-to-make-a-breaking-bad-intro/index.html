The title sequence that made chemistry cool

Breaking Bad's title sequence is a masterclass in economy. Each episode opens with the show's name built from real periodic-table element symbols — Br (Bromine) and Ba (Barium), highlighted in green — set against a time-lapse of New Mexico sky. Simple, clever, and instantly recognizable.

The sequence was created by Mindy Sellar and the team at Electric Sandbox. It took a dry scientific concept (element symbols) and made it feel edgy and dangerous — perfectly capturing the show's theme of a chemistry teacher turned drug manufacturer. When Breaking Bad debuted on AMC in 2008, that title card became one of TV's most distinctive visual identities.

Tips for a great Breaking Bad-style title

  • Pick names with good element symbols — names containing "He" (Helium), "Na" (Sodium), "Ca" (Calcium), "Fe" (Iron), or "Au" (Gold) create striking combinations.
  • Keep it short — the original title is only two words. Shorter names look cleaner and more authentic.
  • Use a dramatic reveal — set your video to play after a long pause or at the end of another clip for maximum impact.

Creative uses for a custom Breaking Bad intro

  • Nicknames and aliases — make someone's nickname look like a dangerous chemical compound.
  • Company or product names — launch something with the gravitas of a Vince Gilligan production.
  • Birthday videos — "[Name]: The [Age]th Element" never gets old.
  • Social media content — Breaking Bad fans love spotting the element symbols. It always gets engagement.
